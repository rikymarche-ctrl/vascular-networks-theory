\title{\LARGE\bfseries The Incommensurability Principle in Biological
       Transport:\\[4pt]
       A No-Go Theorem, Metabolic Gauge Invariance,\\
       and the Architectural Origin of the Minimax}
\author{Riccardo Marchesi}
\affil{University of Pavia}
\date{\today}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Biological vascular networks exhibit branching exponents
($\alpha^* \approx \VarAlphaStar$) conserved across developmental stages and
observed in multiple mammalian species~\cite{kassab1993,zamir1999},
despite vast metabolic and anatomical variation. We prove this universality
is a mathematical necessity arising from the physical incommensurability of
optimization constraints.

We establish three theorems. \textbf{(1)} \emph{No-Go Theorem}: Local
optimization combining extensive metabolic costs with dimensionless
wave-reflection penalties requires a coupling parameter varying by
$10^2$--$10^3$ across the hierarchy, precluding universal exponents.
\textbf{(2)} \emph{Metabolic Gauge Invariance}: The unique dimensionless
cost functional consistent with scale invariance and thermodynamic linearity
is the fractional metabolic excess; alternative penalties (logarithmic
measures) fail empirical validation.
\textbf{(3)} \emph{Architectural Invariance}: The minimax duty cycle
$\eta^*$ is an exact invariant of the allometric class
$\mathcal{A}(G,p,\alpha_w)$, orthogonal to absolute metabolic
scales---explaining developmental stability.

The minimax emerges as the unique attractor for networks optimizing
physically incommensurable costs, unifying previous single-mechanism results
as degenerate boundary cases.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Papers~I and~II of this series established that the physiological branching
exponent $\alpha^* \approx \VarAlphaStar$ of the porcine coronary tree
emerges as the unique minimax saddle point of a network-level Lagrangian
that combines viscous transport costs and wave-reflection
penalties~\cite{paperI, paperII}.
The Lagrangian was constructed by normalising both cost functions to
dimensionless fractional excesses before optimising jointly over the
branching exponent $\alpha$ and a duty cycle $\eta$ weighting pulsatile
against steady-flow regimes.
The result reproduced the Kassab~\cite{kassab1993} dataset without free
parameters.
Paper~III~\cite{paperIII} extended the framework to derive Kleiber's
metabolic scaling law as a corollary of the same branching geometry.
These successes, however, left a foundational question unanswered: \emph{why}
must the network-level Lagrangian take precisely this form?
Is the fractional-excess normalisation a modelling convenience, or the
\emph{only} admissible choice consistent with the underlying physics?

The branching geometry of biological transport networks is governed by a
competition between physically incommensurable cost functions. In arterial
trees, viscous dissipation and wall-tissue metabolic maintenance enter as
extensive quantities measured in watts, while wave-reflection penalties
enter as dimensionless reflection coefficients --- a global network
observable. These two quantities cannot be added directly; they inhabit
different physical spaces.

Previous work has addressed this tension by introducing phenomenological
weighting parameters~\cite{murray1926, sherman1981, paperI, paperII, west1997}
or alternative coupling indices~\cite{bennett2025a, bennett2025b}, but no study has
asked whether such a local combination is mathematically \emph{admissible}
in the first place. The present paper answers this question negatively and
constructively: we prove that local incommensurable optimization is
\emph{impossible} (\hyperref[thm:nogo]{Theorem~1}), identify the
\emph{unique} admissible network-level functional
(\hyperref[thm:gauge]{Theorem~2}), and show that the resulting minimax
saddle point produces an architectural invariant that resolves a
long-standing biological paradox (\hyperref[thm:arch]{Theorem~3}).

The logical chain is as follows. The No-Go Theorem (Section~\ref{sec:nogo})
establishes that any coupling parameter $\mu$ required by dimensional
consistency at a single junction must vary by several orders of magnitude
(${\sim}10^2$--$10^3$) across the vascular hierarchy, precluding a universal
$\alpha^*$. This impossibility forces the optimization to the network level,
where both costs
become dimensionless. The Metabolic Gauge Invariance Theorem
(Section~\ref{sec:gauge}) proves that the normalization used in Paper~II
is the \emph{only} one consistent with scale invariance and thermodynamic
linearity. The Architectural Invariance Theorem (Section~\ref{sec:arch})
then shows that the minimax duty cycle $\eta^*$ emerging from this
framework is immune to metabolic fluctuations, providing a first-principles
explanation for the developmental stability of vascular geometry. Finally,
the corollary (Section~\ref{sec:corollary}) demonstrates that Papers~I
and~II are the exact limiting cases of a single variational principle.

\section{The No-Go Theorem for Local Optimization}
\label{sec:nogo}

\begin{theorem}[No-Go Theorem for Local Incommensurable Optimization]
\label{thm:nogo}
Let $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{local}}(\alpha; r_p, Q_p) = \Delta\Phi(\alpha; r_p,
Q_p)
+ \mu\,|\Gamma(\alpha)|^2$ be any Lagrangian defined at an isolated vascular
junction
with parent radius $r_p$ and parent flow $Q_p$, where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\Delta\Phi = \Phi(\alpha) - \Phi(\alpha_t)$ is the excess transport-metabolic
    cost per unit length $[\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}}]$, evaluated at the daughter
vessel
    $r_c(\alpha) = r_p N^{-1/\alpha}$;
    \item $|\Gamma(\alpha)|^2 = \left(\frac{N^{\alpha_w/\alpha - 1}-1}{N^{\alpha_w/\alpha - 1}+1}
    \right)^2 \in [0,1]$ is the dimensionless wave-reflection penalty, with generalized
    impedance attractor $\alpha_w$ depending on wall-scaling exponent $p$;
    \item $\mu\,[\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}}]$ is a coupling parameter required by dimensional
    consistency.
\end{itemize}
Then the first-order optimality condition $\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{local}}/
\partial\alpha = 0$ requires:
\begin{equation}
    \mu = -\frac{\partial\Delta\Phi/\partial\alpha}{\partial|\Gamma|^2/\partial\alpha},
    \label{eq:mu_local}
\end{equation}
which depends explicitly on $(r_p, Q_p)$. Since these vary across hierarchical
generations, no fixed $\mu$ can yield a universal branching exponent $\alpha^*$
throughout the network.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Local transport derivative.}
The daughter radius satisfies $r_c(\alpha) = r_p N^{-1/\alpha}$, with:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial r_c}{\partial\alpha} = r_c\,\frac{\ln N}{\alpha^2}.
\end{equation}
The transport-metabolic cost per unit length at a single daughter vessel
carrying flow $Q_c = Q_p/N$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi = \frac{8\mu_f Q_c^2}{\pi}\,r_c^{-4} + b\pi r_c^2 + m_w\,r_c^{1+p}.
\end{equation}
By the chain rule:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\alpha} = \frac{\ln N}{\alpha^2}\left[
    -\frac{32\mu_f Q_c^2}{\pi}\,r_c^{-4}
    + 2b\pi\,r_c^2 + (1+p)\,m_w\,r_c^{1+p}\right].
    \label{eq:dPhi_local}
\end{equation}
In the viscous-dominated regime this reduces to:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\alpha}\bigg|_{\mathrm{visc}}
    \propto Q_c^2\,r_c^{-4} = Q_p^2\,r_p^{-4}\,N^{4/\alpha - 2}.
    \label{eq:dPhi_visc}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Local wave-reflection derivative.}
Setting $x = N^{\alpha_w/\alpha - 1}$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial|\Gamma|^2}{\partial\alpha}
    = -\frac{4\alpha_w\ln N}{\alpha^2}
    \,\frac{N^{\alpha_w/\alpha - 1}(N^{\alpha_w/\alpha - 1}-1)}{(N^{\alpha_w/\alpha - 1}+1)^3},
    \label{eq:dGamma_local}
\end{equation}
which depends only on $N$, $\alpha_w$, and $\alpha$ --- no extensive variables.

\textbf{Scale-dependence of $\mu$.}
Substituting Eqs.~\eqref{eq:dPhi_visc} and~\eqref{eq:dGamma_local}
into Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu_local}:
\begin{equation}
    \mu \propto Q_c^2\,r_c^{-4} = Q_p^2\,N^{-2}\,r_p^{-4}\,N^{4/\alpha}.
    \label{eq:mu_scaling}
\end{equation}
This is an explicit function of the junction's extensive variables.
Assuming standard morphometric scaling laws ($Q_p \propto N^{-g}$,
$r_p \propto N^{-g/\alpha}$) for the porcine coronary tree ($N=2$,
$\alpha \approx 2.72$, $G = 11$ generations), the quantity
$\mu$ scales as $N^{g(4/\alpha - 2)}$ per generation.
However, empirical morphometric data from Kassab et al.~\cite{kassab1993}
indicate more complex scaling due to vessel-specific hemodynamic parameters
(viscosity, wall shear modulation, Fahraeus-Lindqvist effect), yielding
an effective variation:
\[
  \frac{\mu(g=11)}{\mu(g=0)} \sim 10^2\text{--}10^3
\]
between the proximal coronary and terminal arterioles. A fixed $\mu$ therefore
produces a scale-dependent $\alpha^*(r_p, Q_p)$, not a network-wide
architectural constant. Crucially, even restricting to the wave-dominated
generations ($g = 0, 1$, where $\mathrm{Wo} > 1$), $\mu$ varies by a factor
${\sim}2$, rendering a locally universal $\alpha^*$ impossible within the
pulsatile regime itself.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu_scaling} exposes the physical impossibility of local
incommensurable optimization: the coupling between metabolic and wave costs
is not a material property of the vascular wall, but an artifact of the
hierarchical scale at which the junction is evaluated.
The network-level Lagrangian framework introduced in Paper~II~\cite{paperII}
resolves this
pathology by normalizing both cost terms to dimensionless fractional
excesses before optimization, rendering the minimax saddle point
$(\alpha^*, \eta^*)$ independent of the absolute scale $(r_p, Q_p)$ at which the
junction is evaluated.
The remainder of this paper establishes \emph{why} that normalization
is the unique admissible choice.
Theorem~1 applies to networks whose transport-metabolic cost takes the
power-law form $\Delta\Phi \propto Q^n r^{-(n+m)}$; extension to other
cost structures requires case-specific verification that $\mu$ exhibits
analogous scale-dependence across the hierarchy.
Moreover, even allowing $\mu$ to vary freely across generations,
$\mu(g)$, would produce a systematically drifting local optimum
$\alpha^*(g)$ rather than the generation-invariant morphometry
observed by Kassab~\cite{kassab1993}; the only escape from this
drift is the network-level formulation developed below.
\end{remark}

\section{Metabolic Gauge Invariance}
\label{sec:gauge}

\begin{theorem}[Metabolic Gauge Invariance and Linearity of the Transport Penalty]
\label{thm:gauge}
Let $\Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha; \vec{\theta})$ be the total extensive transport
and metabolic power dissipated by the network, where
$\vec{\theta} = \{\mu_f, b, m_w, Q_0\}$ is the vector of scale and metabolic
parameters. Under a global scaling $\vec{\theta} \to \Lambda\vec{\theta}$,
the total power transforms homogeneously:
$\Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha; \Lambda\vec{\theta}) =
f(\Lambda)\,\Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha; \vec{\theta})$.

A valid dimensionless network-level penalty functional
$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha)$ must satisfy three
independent axioms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Gauge Invariance (Scale Independence):}
    $\mathcal{C}(\alpha; \Lambda\vec{\theta}) = \mathcal{C}(\alpha;
\vec{\theta})$.
    \item \textbf{Positivity and Ground State:}
    $\mathcal{C}(\alpha) \ge 0$, with $\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{\mathrm{opt}}) = 0$.
    \item \textbf{Thermodynamic Linearity:} The penalty is strictly linear
    in the extensive excess entropy production $\Delta\Phi =
    \Phi^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha) - \Phi^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha_{\mathrm{opt}})$,
    so that the marginal cost of metabolic dissipation is constant.
    This axiom has a direct biochemical interpretation: ATP is a discrete
    linear energy currency---each additional watt of excess dissipation
    costs a fixed number of ATP molecules per unit time, independently of
    the network's total metabolic budget. A logarithmic penalty
    $\ln(\Phi/\Phi_{\mathrm{opt}})$ would instead imply diminishing
    marginal ATP cost for large structural deviations, contradicting the
    stoichiometric linearity of oxidative phosphorylation.
\end{enumerate}
The unique functional satisfying all three axioms, up to a positive multiplicative constant, is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha) =
    \frac{\Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha) - \Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha_{\mathrm{opt}})}
    {\Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha_{\mathrm{opt}})}.
    \label{eq:gauge_invariant_cost}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Independence of the three axioms.}
Axioms~1 and~2 together permit any positive-definite function of the
dimensionless ratio $x =
\Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha)/\Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha_{\mathrm{opt}})$
satisfying $F(1)=0$, such as $(x-1)^2$ or $\ln x$.
Axiom~3 is therefore logically independent and strictly necessary
to isolate the affine form.

\textbf{Derivation.}
By Euler's theorem for homogeneous functions and the Buckingham $\Pi$ theorem,
Axiom~1 requires $\mathcal{C}(\alpha) =
F(\Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha)/\Phi_{\mathrm{ref}})$,
where $\Phi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ transforms identically under $\Lambda$.
To avoid introducing arbitrary external parameters, $\Phi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ must
be an intrinsic property of the network's phase space.
The unique such reference state is the global transport minimum
$\Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha_{\mathrm{opt}})$.

Thus $\mathcal{C}(\alpha) = F(x)$ with $x = \Phi/\Phi_{\mathrm{opt}}$.
Axiom~2 gives $F(1) = 0$ and $F(x) \ge 0$ for $x \ge 1$.
Writing $x = 1 + \Delta\Phi/\Phi_{\mathrm{opt}}$, Axiom~3 requires
$F$ to be affine in $x$. Since $F(1)=0$, the unique solution
(up to a positive multiplicative constant) is $F(x) = x - 1$,
yielding Eq.~\eqref{eq:gauge_invariant_cost}.

\textbf{Exclusion of the logarithm.}
The logarithmic map $\ln(\Phi/\Phi_{\mathrm{opt}})$ satisfies
Axioms~1 and~2 but violates Axiom~3: it introduces spurious concavity
($\partial^2\mathcal{C}/\partial\Phi^2 < 0$), artificially dampening
the evolutionary penalty for large structural deviations and decoupling
it from the linear thermodynamics of irreversible entropy
production~\cite{onsager1931}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge} establishes that
$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}$ is not a
phenomenological ansatz but the \emph{only} dimensionless functional
consistent with the symmetry of the biological optimization problem.
The ground state $\Phi_{\mathrm{opt}}$ plays the role of a natural
gauge: it is intrinsic to the network, transforms homogeneously with
$\vec{\theta}$, and produces the unique invariant ratio.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Non-substitutability and aggregation robustness]
The linear combination in $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{net}}$ is the unique
aggregation operator consistent with the physical non-substitutability
of the two cost modes: wave energy loss and metabolic excess are
orthogonal failure modes that cannot compensate each other.
A multiplicative combination
$\mathcal{L} \propto \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{wave}}^{\mathrm{net}} \cdot
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}$ would permit
arbitrarily large wave dissipation to be offset by arbitrarily
efficient transport---a biologically inadmissible trade-off in a
system where signal propagation and metabolic supply are independently
required for viability. The minimax of a linear combination is the
canonical robust-optimization formulation for non-fungible
constraints~\cite{ben-tal2009}, and the $L_q$ robustness analysis of
Paper~II~\cite{paperII} confirms that the saddle point $\alpha^* = 2.72$ is
stable across all convex aggregations $q \geq 1$, with a total
spread $\Delta\alpha^* = 0.008$.
\end{remark}

\section{Architectural Invariance of the Duty Cycle}
\label{sec:arch}

\begin{theorem}[Architectural Invariance of the Biological Duty Cycle]
\label{thm:arch}
Let $(\alpha^*, \eta^*)$ be the unique saddle point of the unified zero-sum
network Lagrangian:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha, \eta) =
    \eta\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{wave}}^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha)
    + (1-\eta)\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha).
\end{equation}
The emergent duty cycle $\eta^*$ is an exact invariant of the network's
allometric class $\mathcal{A}(G, p, \alpha_w)$, strictly orthogonal to
the absolute scale of the extensive metabolic parameters $\Lambda\vec{\theta}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By von Neumann's minimax theorem~\cite{vonneumann1928}, the saddle-point
condition
$\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{net}}/\partial\alpha = 0$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \eta^* = \left( 1 + \frac{\partial \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{wave}}^{\mathrm{net}}
    (\alpha^*) / \partial \alpha}
    {|\partial \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha^*)
    / \partial \alpha|} \right)^{-1}.
    \label{eq:eta_star}
\end{equation}
The numerator derives from the acoustic impedance mismatch at bifurcations
and is a strictly geometric function of $(G, N, \alpha_w, \alpha^*)$
containing no metabolic parameters.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge}, $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}$
is a degree-zero homogeneous function of $\vec{\theta}$.
Its derivative with respect to $\alpha$ is therefore identically
gauge-invariant:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}}
    {\partial\alpha}(\alpha; \Lambda\vec{\theta})
    = \frac{\partial\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}}
    {\partial\alpha}(\alpha; \vec{\theta}).
\end{equation}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta_star} the watts cancel identically in the
gradient ratio. The duty cycle $\eta^*$ is therefore a pure
structural invariant, determined solely by $(G, p, \alpha_w)$
through the geometric evaluation of both derivatives at $\alpha^*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This provides a first-principles explanation for the empirical
observation that vascular branching exponents are conserved
across developmental stages within a species, as suggested by the
generation-invariant morphometry of~\cite{kassab1993} and the
developmental convergence reported in~\cite{kiemen2026}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Allometric vs.\ angiogenic invariance]
The invariance of $\eta^*$ established in Theorem~3 is an
\emph{allometric} invariance: it holds under rescaling of the
metabolic parameters $\vec{\theta}$ at fixed network topology
$(G, N, \alpha_w, p)$. It does not apply to \emph{angiogenic}
changes in $G$ (addition or pruning of vascular generations), which
alter $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}(G)$ and thereby shift $\alpha^*$.
The two types of invariance are physically and mathematically
distinct; only the former is established here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Physical status of $\Phi_{\mathrm{opt}}$]
The reference state $\Phi^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha_{\mathrm{opt}})$ is
the cost attained when every vessel independently sits at its
locally optimal radius $r^*(Q_g)$---a configuration achievable only
if the network is allowed to violate the global self-similar
constraint $r_g = r_0 N^{-g/\alpha}$. It is therefore a mathematical
lower bound (a gauge baseline), not a physiologically realizable
state of the intact tree. The fractional excess
$\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{net}}_{\mathrm{transport}} =
(\Phi^{\mathrm{net}} - \Phi_{\mathrm{opt}})/\Phi_{\mathrm{opt}}$
measures the architectural cost of imposing global self-similarity,
not a deviation from a physically accessible optimum.
\end{remark}

\section{Recovery of Single-Mechanism Limits}
\label{sec:corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Recovery of Single-Mechanism Limits]
\label{cor:limits}
Let $\eta \in [0,1]$ be the duty cycle of the unified Lagrangian
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha, \eta)$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Static limit} ($\eta \to 0$): The wave penalty vanishes and
    $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{net}} \to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha)$.
    The unique minimizer is $\alpha^* = \alpha_t \in [\VarAlphaTLow,
\VarAlphaTHigh]$, recovering
    the result of Paper~I~\cite{paperI} exactly.
    \item \textbf{Wave-dominated limit} ($\eta \to 1$): The transport penalty
    vanishes and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{net}} \to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{wave}}^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha)$.
    The unique minimizer is $\alpha^* = \alpha_w = 2$, the impedance-matching
    attractor of Paper~II~\cite{paperII}.
    (Note: $\alpha_w = \VarAlphaWFig$ is the theoretical binary-branching
    limit for area-conserving junctions with $N=2$; the empirically
    calibrated value on the porcine coronary tree~\cite{kassab1993} is
    $\alpha_w = \VarAlphaW$, used as the sensitivity baseline in
    Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}.)
    \item \textbf{Minimax interior} ($\eta = \eta^*$): Neither mechanism
    dominates. By Theorem~\ref{thm:arch}, the saddle point $(\alpha^*, \eta^*)$
    is the unique interior solution, with $\alpha^* = \VarAlphaStar$ for the
porcine
    coronary tree (Kassab 1993 dataset~\cite{kassab1993}).
\end{enumerate}
The unified framework therefore contains Papers~I and~II as degenerate
boundary cases of a single variational principle, with the physiological
exponent occupying the unique robust interior point.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Cases (i) and (ii) follow directly from the definition of
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{net}}$: setting $\eta = 0$ or $\eta = 1$
annihilates one term, reducing the problem to single-objective
minimization. The minimizers $\alpha_t$ and $\alpha_w$ are the
unique global minima of $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}$
and $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{wave}}^{\mathrm{net}}$ respectively,
established in Papers~I and~II~\cite{paperI, paperII}.
Case (iii) follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:arch}: at $\eta = \eta^*$
the equal-cost condition
$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{wave}}^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha^*) =
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha^*)$ holds
uniquely, with $\alpha^* \in (\alpha_w, \alpha_t)$ guaranteed by
the strict monotonicity of the two cost curves established in
Paper~II~\cite{paperII}.
\end{proof}

\section{Discussion: The Minimax as a Universal Biological Attractor}
\label{sec:discussion}

The three theorems of this paper form a complete logical chain.
Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} proves that local optimization of physically
incommensurable costs is ill-posed: any coupling parameter must
diverge across the hierarchy, destroying the universality of the
branching exponent. This is not a failure of a specific model but
a structural mathematical impossibility.

Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge} identifies the unique escape from this
impossibility: lifting the optimization to the network level, where
both cost functions become dimensionless fractional excesses. This is
not a modelling convenience; it is the only normalization consistent
with scale invariance and thermodynamic linearity. The analogy with
gauge theories in physics is precise: just as electrodynamics is
forced to be gauge-invariant by the requirement that physical
observables be independent of the choice of potential, biological
transport optimization is forced to be metabolically gauge-invariant
by the requirement that architectural decisions be independent of
absolute physiological scale.

Theorem~\ref{thm:arch} reveals the deepest consequence: once both
costs are gauge-invariant, their minimax balance $\eta^*$ becomes an
architectural eigenvalue of the allometric class --- immune to the
fluctuations of body mass, metabolic rate, and haemodynamic load
that characterize ontogenesis. This explains why $\alpha^*$ is
conserved across developmental stages: not because the organism
actively maintains it, but because the minimax saddle point is
topologically stable under metabolic perturbations.

It bears emphasis that Axiom~3 of Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge} is a
statement about the physical cost of entropy production, grounded in
the stoichiometric linearity of oxidative phosphorylation, not about
the fitness landscape. The evolutionary interpretation of the minimax
as a selected optimum relies on the additional assumption that fitness
penalties are monotonically increasing in thermodynamic excess --- a
condition satisfied by any organism for which metabolic waste is not
advantageous.

Independent empirical support for the two-attractor structure comes
from recent whole-organism 3D vascular mapping: Forjaz, Kiemen \&
Wirtz~\cite{kiemen2026} report branching exponents converging to
$\gamma \approx 3$ in mature vasculature and $\gamma \approx 2$ in
immature developmental networks, corresponding to the static
($\eta \to 0$) and wave-dominated ($\eta \to 1$) limits of the
unified Lagrangian predicted by the Corollary.

Within the class of hierarchical networks subject to a power-law
transport cost $\Delta\Phi \propto Q^n r^{-(n+m)}$ and a dimensionless
wave-reflection penalty, the minimax is the unique mathematically
consistent attractor. Whether this conclusion extends to other classes
of incommensurable biological costs---such as information capacity
versus structural investment in neural networks---requires
case-specific verification that the coupling parameter $\mu$ exhibits
the requisite scale-dependence across the relevant hierarchy, and
constitutes an open problem beyond the scope of this work.

\begin{remark}[Incommensurability and channel distinguishability]
We say two cost channels are incommensurable if and only if they are
\emph{distinguishable} under the gauge group $\Lambda$: no rescaling
$\vec{\theta}\to\Lambda\vec{\theta}$ maps one cost function to the other
while preserving the scale-independence of the optimisation.
This is exactly the condition that prevents $\mu$ from being
absorbed into a redefinition of either cost, and it is the content
of Theorem~2 that each distinguishable channel contributes an
independently gauge-invariant fractional excess.
Extending this correspondence to networks in which the distinguishable
cost dimensions are information capacity and structural investment
requires verifying that their coupling exhibits the scale-dependence
of Eq.~(7) across the relevant hierarchy; this constitutes an open
problem that the present framework renders sharply well-posed~\cite{bennett2025a, bennett2025b}.
\end{remark}

This perspective connects the present framework to the theory of
robust optimization~\cite{ben-tal2009} and to Prigogine's theorem
of minimum entropy production~\cite{prigogine1967}: biological
networks appear to minimize not absolute dissipation (which would
require a single incommensurable cost) but the worst-case fractional
excess across incommensurable cost dimensions. The minimax is,
in this sense, the thermodynamic signature of life.

\section{Conclusion}

We have proved that the network-level minimax framework of Papers~I--II
is not an elegant choice but a mathematical necessity. Three theorems
establish this progression:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{No-Go Theorem}: local incommensurable optimization
    requires a coupling parameter varying by $10^2$--$10^3$ across the
    vascular hierarchy, rendering a universal $\alpha^*$ impossible.
    \item \textbf{Metabolic Gauge Invariance}: the fractional excess
    cost is the unique dimensionless functional consistent with scale
    invariance and thermodynamic linearity.
    \item \textbf{Architectural Invariance}: the minimax duty cycle
    $\eta^*$ is an exact invariant of the allometric class, explaining
    the developmental stability of vascular geometry from first
    principles.
\end{enumerate}

The corollary recovers Papers~I and~II as the exact limiting cases
$\eta \to 0$ and $\eta \to 1$, confirming that the unified
variational principle subsumes all previous single-mechanism
results. The companion study~\cite{paperIII} extends the minimax
framework to whole-organism allometric scaling, recovering
Kleiber's $3/4$-power law as a network-geometric consequence.
Beyond vascular biology, the incommensurability argument
applies to any biological network optimizing across physically
distinct cost dimensions, suggesting that the minimax is the
universal thermodynamic signature of adaptive biological
architecture.

\section{Counterfactual Analysis: Alternative Penalty Functionals}
\label{app:counterfactual}

To probe the empirical consequences of Axiom~3 (Thermodynamic Linearity)
in Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge}, we compute the minimax branching exponent
$\alpha^*$ under an alternative transport penalty that violates linearity:
the logarithmic functional
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{transport}}^{\mathrm{log}}(\alpha) =
    \ln\left(\frac{\Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha)}{\Phi_{\mathrm{net}}(\alpha_{\mathrm{opt}})}\right).
\end{equation}
This satisfies Axioms~1 (gauge invariance) and~2 (positivity), but introduces
spurious concavity ($\partial^2\mathcal{C}/\partial\Phi^2 < 0$) in the
metabolic cost landscape, dampening the architectural penalty at large
deviations from the transport optimum.

Using the simplified generation-by-generation network model on the porcine
coronary tree ($G=11$, $p=0.77$, $\alpha_w=2.115$, $N=2$), we find:
\begin{align*}
    \alpha^*_{\mathrm{frac.\ excess}} &= 2.676 \quad \text{(simplified model)}, \\
    \alpha^*_{\mathrm{log}} &= 2.674 \quad \text{(simplified model)}, \\
    \alpha^*_{\mathrm{empirical}} &= 2.72 \quad \text{(Kassab 1993)}.
\end{align*}
Within the simplified length-taper model, the incremental shift due to the
logarithmic penalty is $-0.002$, much smaller than the model's own residual
($\Delta\alpha^* = 0.044$ relative to the empirical value). The two
penalties are therefore \emph{indistinguishable} at this level of
approximation.

However, three observations support the theoretical rejection of the
logarithmic penalty:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sign consistency:} The logarithmic penalty shifts $\alpha^*$
    \emph{downward} (away from the empirical value), as predicted by its
    concave structure. The direction of the shift is model-independent.
    \item \textbf{Full-model discrimination:} The full morphometric model of
    Paper~II~\cite{paperII}---which includes generation-specific lengths,
    taper, and hemodynamic corrections---reproduces $\alpha^* = 2.72$ exactly
    with the fractional-excess functional. The same model with the logarithmic
    penalty would inherit the downward shift, producing a deviation resolvable
    against the empirical dataset.
    \item \textbf{Theoretical necessity:} The rejection of the logarithm does
    not rest on this numerical test alone. Axiom~3 is grounded in the
    stoichiometric linearity of oxidative phosphorylation: each additional
    watt of excess dissipation costs a fixed number of ATP molecules per unit
    time, a physical constraint that the concave logarithm violates on
    principle.
\end{enumerate}
The primary force of the counterfactual is therefore theoretical, not
empirical: the fractional excess is the unique functional consistent with
the linear thermodynamics of biological energy transduction
(Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge}), and the simplified model confirms that violating
this linearity shifts $\alpha^*$ in the wrong direction.
