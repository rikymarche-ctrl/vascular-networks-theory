\noindent\textbf{Comment 1 --- Opening contrast (mouse vs.\ whale heart rate).}
\begin{comment}
The introduction motivates the problem by contrasting the mouse
($\sim$600~bpm) and the whale ($\sim$20~bpm) heart rates. Heart rate alone is a
misleading basis for the comparison; the relevant quantity is the volume of
blood each heart pumps (cardiac output / stroke volume). The argument on the
Womersley number should be reformulated accordingly and checked for
consistency.
\end{comment}

\response We agree. The correction sharpens the physical logic and, in fact,
repairs a potential inversion of the intended point. Because the Womersley number $\mathrm{Wo} = R\sqrt{\omega/\nu}$
\emph{increases} with frequency, a comparison on heart rate alone would make the
mouse appear \emph{more} inertial than the whale, the opposite of our thesis.

We have rewritten the opening contrast in terms of the pumped volume. Cardiac
output scales as $\dot{Q}\propto M^{3/4}$ and stroke volume as $\propto M$, so a
single whale contraction ejects a blood volume larger than the mouse's by about
seven orders of magnitude. That volume is carried by vessels whose radius scales
as $R\propto M^{3/8}$, and it is the radius---not the rapid murine heart
rate---that dominates the Womersley number. Combining the allometric scalings
$R\propto M^{3/8}$ and $f\propto M^{-1/4}$ gives
\[
  \mathrm{Wo}_0 \;\propto\; M^{3/8}\,\sqrt{M^{-1/4}}
  \;=\; M^{3/8}\,M^{-1/8} \;=\; M^{1/4},
\]
i.e.\ the Womersley number \emph{rises} with body mass despite the slower
large-animal heart beat. This resolves precisely the apparent paradox the
reviewer identified.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item The revision is \emph{additive}: the original sentence is preserved and

  \item A cross-reference was added to the identical derivation already present
        in Section~6 (\texttt{sec:womersley\_minimax}).
  \item The cardiac-output scaling $\dot{Q}\propto M^{3/4}$ used in the new
        passage is now supported by an explicit citation to Noujaim et al.,
        \emph{Circulation} \textbf{110} (2004) 2802 --- a study titled, fittingly,
        ``From mouse to whale'' --- which documents the quarter-power allometry of
        mammalian cardiac timing and output across the same mass range.
  \item Internal-consistency check: the $M^{1/4}$ law predicts a human/mouse
        Womersley ratio of $(70000/20)^{1/4}\approx 7.7$, matching the
        Section~6 table ($\approx 7.2$) to within $\sim$7\%.
  \item To avoid a residual ambiguity, a short author clarification (magenta in
        the highlighted copy) was added immediately afterward so that the mouse
        is \emph{not} read as realising Murray's $\alpha=3$ architecture: the
        mouse and whale cases are framed as the two single-mechanism limits
        (viscous $\alpha_t$ and wave $\alpha_w\approx 2$) of the unified
        Lagrangian, consistent with Sections~5--6 (where the mouse coronary is
        predicted in the transition range, not at $\alpha=3$).
\end{itemize}
